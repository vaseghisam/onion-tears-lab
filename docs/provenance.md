# Provenance and scope

This package accompanies Sam Vaseghi's article in *The Serious Science of Annoyances*. Version 1.0.0 was prepared on 24 September 2026 through an AI-assisted research, computation, and editorial workflow at the author's request.

The coordinating assistant developed the article and integrated the package. Separate AI agents researched primary sources, implemented the model, independently checked its mathematics and numerical outputs, produced the visuals, and reviewed scientific claims. Their reports are included in `qa/`. Independent review here means separate work within this AI team; it is not external peer review, a laboratory replication, or a human scientific endorsement.

Version 1.1.0 integrates the author's revised manuscript of 27 September 2026. Its model source, configuration and saved numerical results remain identical to version 1.0.0. Production changes comprise figure numbering and paths, local copies of supplied images, the air-motion table, Markdown formatting, package metadata and the agreed access statement. See `docs/publication_update.md`.

## What was produced

The Python calculations, numerical tables, diagrams, plots, and animation in this package were produced for this project. The results are generated by an explicitly specified idealized transport model. They contain no experimental measurements from the author, no measurements of a particular kitchen, and no human-subject data.

The biochemical and physiological discussion cites published primary research. The source ledger records what was accessible, the relevant source locations, and the limits of each claim. A targeted literature search is not an exhaustive systematic review. Failure to locate a controlled trial is not proof that a remedy has no effect.

No empirical calibration is claimed for source time constants, source width, air velocity, or effective mixing coefficient. Parameters marked illustrative are chosen scenarios. Alternative parameter calculations measure sensitivity within the model; their ranges are not population distributions or confidence intervals.

## Third-party materials

Version 1.1.0 includes the featured painting and two research illustrations supplied through links in the author's final manuscript. Their unmodified image bytes, original URLs, source credits and checksums are recorded in `assets/asset_sources.json`. Journal PDFs, experimental movies and investigators' original datasets are not bundled. The research files link to primary publications and, where available, their data repositories. The original onion-cutting data and MATLAB code of Wu and colleagues are available from the repository cited in their PNAS paper: [OSF project](https://osf.io/5xady/).

The project's plots visualize its own saved calculations. The chemistry figure is an original schematic of a cited reaction sequence, not a reproduction of a publisher's figure. It does not assert molecular geometry, reaction yields, or cellular dimensions.

## Version and reproducibility

`MANIFEST.sha256` records SHA-256 hashes of the released files, except the manifest itself. The reproduction instructions identify the tested Python dependencies and the external FFmpeg requirement. Fresh output can be compared numerically; archive timestamps and video/container metadata need not be identical across machines.

This packaging step does not create, publish or change access to a GitHub repository. The author's policy is to keep the repository private and consider access requests individually. No GitHub URL, repository DOI or external peer-review status is assumed.

No general reuse licence has been selected for the article, original assets, or code in this package. Third-party works retain their own terms.
